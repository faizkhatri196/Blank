# Empirical AI Evaluation Framework

## 1. Principles of Truthful AI Evaluation

To build durable enterprise software for industrial operations, AI Field Service Copilot adheres to three strict principles:
1. **Never Fake Analytics**: Metrics are calculated directly from Mongoose database aggregations of completed tickets and verified service reports. Fractions are always presented alongside percentages (e.g., `7 / 10 (70%)`).
2. **Deterministic Matching**: AI vs Actual comparisons use standardized fuzzy token and keyword intersection rather than subjective model self-grading.
3. **Technician Primacy**: The technician's physical observations on site are treated as ground truth. If the AI predicted an inverter capacitor failure but the technician found a cut DC cable, the AI is marked as **unmatched**, and the ground data is stored to retrain the prompt context.

## 2. Match Definitions & Formulas

### 2.1. Root Cause Prediction Match (`issuePredictionMatched`)
- **Condition**: The technician's `actualRootCause` contains key diagnostic keywords that intersect with any of the `possibleIssues[].issue` or `possibleIssues[].rootCauses[]` generated by the AI.
- **Normalization**: Text is lowercased, stripped of punctuation, and evaluated against vertical-specific synonym dictionaries (e.g., "grid surge", "overvoltage", "tripped breaker").

### 2.2. Parts Prediction Match (`partsPredictionMatched`)
- **Condition**: If the technician used replacement parts (`partsUsed.length > 0`), at least one physical part name matches an entry in `expectedParts[].partName`.
- **Exclusion**: If no replacement parts were required for the repair (e.g., simple cleaning, wire tightening, breaker reset) and the AI anticipated zero parts or low-probability parts, it is recorded as a clean operational fix.

### 2.3. Tools Match Rate (`toolsMatchRate`)
$$\text{Tools Match Rate} = \frac{\text{Required Tools in AI Suggestions} \cap \text{Tools Actually Used}}{\text{Tools Actually Used}} \times 100\%$$
A low tools match rate indicates that the AI failed to warn the technician to bring a specialized tool (e.g., thermal imaging camera or nitrogen pressure test kit).

### 2.4. Duration Variance (`durationVarianceMinutes`)
$$\Delta_{\text{duration}} = \text{Actual Labor Minutes} - \text{AI Predicted Labor Minutes}$$
Tracks whether the AI under- or over-estimates physical job complexity.

## 3. CSV Export Structure

When exporting metrics from the Pilot Insights tab (`GET /api/pilot/export-csv`), the resulting CSV provides 22 audit columns:

1. `Ticket Number` (e.g., `SOL-2026-0001`)
2. `Service Type` (`SOLAR` / `AC_HVAC`)
3. `Priority` (`LOW` / `MEDIUM` / `HIGH` / `CRITICAL`)
4. `Status` (`COMPLETED`)
5. `Customer Name`
6. `Customer Phone`
7. `Customer Address`
8. `Asset Details` (Capacity, Inverter, Model, Refrigerant)
9. `Complaint Description`
10. `AI Provider` (`GEMINI`, `GROQ`, or `DEMO`)
11. `AI Predicted Issues`
12. `AI Expected Parts`
13. `AI Predicted Duration (Min)`
14. `Assigned Technician`
15. `Actual Root Cause`
16. `Work Performed`
17. `Parts Used`
18. `Actual Labor (Min)`
19. `Physical Readings` (JSON string of voltages/pressures)
20. `Issue Matched?` (`YES` / `NO`)
21. `Parts Matched?` (`YES` / `NO`)
22. `Technician AI Rating` (1–5)
